Key findings

Finding 1
ρ = +0.11
Morphological distance does not predict method-ranking agreement. Spearman correlation between morphological distance and Kendall-τ rank agreement across all 66 case pairs: p = 0.38.
Finding 2
ρ = +0.61 *
L-skewness is the strongest predictor of overall case difficulty (mean MASE). Right-skewed series — those with occasional large spikes — are harder to forecast on average. Significant at p = 0.036.
Finding 3 — strongest
ρ = +0.77 **
exog_r2_gain strongly predicts the MASE improvement from adding exogenous variables to LightGBM (p = 0.004). The feature does what it promises.
Bottom line

Morphological similarity remains a weak global predictor of shared method rankings — cases that look alike do not systematically prefer the same methods. Under the revised feature set, seasonal_strength is no longer a significant difficulty signal (ρ = −0.40, p = 0.20); the difficulty signal that survives is l_skewness, which tracks mean MASE (right-skewed, spiky series are harder). The headline result is unchanged and slightly stronger: exog_r2_gain reliably flags when exogenous-aware methods will outperform their univariate counterparts (ρ = +0.77). Separately, the benchmark itself is decisive — every one of the 12 cases is won by an exogenous-aware ML method or by Chronos-Bolt, never by a pure univariate baseline. The sample (n = 12) is underpowered, so null results should be read as "inconclusive" rather than "no effect."
